3. In cricket terms: what this tells us

Written by the language model · every number from code
Takeaway 1

The run rate alone barely helps

The TV's projection missed by 22.9 runs, hardly better than knowing nothing at all (24.6). How fast a side has scored says little about where it will finish.

Takeaway 2

Wickets in hand are worth runs

Each wicket lost by the halfway mark costs about 6.5 runs by the end. What moves the prediction most, for a typical difference:

runs at 10+14
wickets lost−11

Takeaway 3

Sides speed up, and the TV forgets it

Teams score faster in the last 10 overs than the first 10, so the TV's straight line guessed 14 runs too low on average. The models learned that from past innings.

Rule of thumb

Next time the TV shows a projected score at the halfway mark, add about 14 runs. Add a bit more if wickets are in hand, a bit less if they're not.
