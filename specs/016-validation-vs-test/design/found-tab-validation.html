What the agent found

Two methods competed to pick the best clues for the prediction. One thinks like an analyst, the other searches like a machine. This is how each one searched, and which setup goes forward to the final test.

VALIDATION

Every score on this tab comes from the check seasons, 2023 to 2025 (cross-validation). They are used to choose a setup, not to judge it. The honest verdict comes from the final test.

1. The contest: two ways to choose the clues

The AI agent a language model · up to 6 tries

Thinks like an analyst. It reads the results so far, uses what it knows about cricket, and proposes the next setup to try, saying why.

Code checks every proposal and does all the fitting and scoring. The AI never supplies a number.

The rival: forward selection plain code · 24 setups

Searches like a machine. For every combination of settings it starts with no clues, adds whichever single clue cuts the miss most, and repeats until nothing helps.

No judgement, no cricket knowledge: just trying things in order.

Why have a rival? It's the yardstick. If the AI can't beat a simple mechanical search, its reasoning isn't adding anything.

What's a setup? The clues (features), how many past seasons to learn from, whether recent seasons count more, and which innings to learn from.

2. How every setup is scored: three check seasons (cross-validation)

Each setup learns from earlier seasons, then predicts a check season it hasn't seen, three times over. Its score is its average miss across the three, in runs. Lower is better. These scores are used again and again to compare setups, which is exactly why they can't also be the final verdict.
